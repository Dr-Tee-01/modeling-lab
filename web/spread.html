Spread Lab

differential equations · Monte Carlo · live in the browser

Six models of how things move through a population: a drug through the bloodstream, a product through a market, flu through a school, a rumour through a crowd, decay through a sample of atoms, and a walker through space. Every curve on this page is computed as you drag the sliders.

Pharmacokinetics

Keep a drug inside its therapeutic window

Each dose lands on top of whatever the body has not cleared yet. Tune the dose, interval and half-life and watch whether blood levels settle between the minimum effective and maximum safe concentration. Defaults follow phenytoin (Dilantin): 22 h half-life, window 10–20 µg/mL.


      

      

        

        

          

          
          
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Product adoption · Bass model
    
          
    Advertising starts it, word of mouth finishes it
    
          
    Every non-adopter buys at rate p because of outside influence, plus q times the share of the market that already owns the product. The S-curve, the sales peak and the split between innovators and imitators all follow from those two numbers.
    
        
    
        
    modeldN/dt = (p + q·N/C)(C − N)
closed formN(t) = C(1 − e^−(p+q)t) / (1 + (q/p)e^−(p+q)t)
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
      
        
      
      
      
    
      

    
    
      
      
      
        
      
          
      Epidemics · SIR
      
          
      Flu in a boarding school, and what a vaccine or a lockdown would have changed

dI/dt =  β·S·I/N − α·I

        Information spread · ISR model
        
          
        How far a rumour travels before people tire of it
        
          
        Ignorants hear the rumour from spreaders. Spreaders stop when they meet someone who already knows it, so a rumour is slowed by its own success. An education campaign (α) moves ignorants straight to stiflers; Λ adds newcomers who have not heard it.
        
        
        
        
        modeldI/dt = Λ − β·S·I/N − α·I
dS/dt = β·S·I/N − γ·S·(S+R)/N
dR/dt = γ·S·(S+R)/N + α·I     R₀ = 1 + β/γ
        
      
        
      
        
        
        
        
        
          
        
          
        
            
            
          
        
          
          
        
          
      
          
    
          

    
    
          
      
          
        
          
          
          Monte Carlo
          
          
          Randomness that averages out
          
          
          A thousand atoms, each with the same small chance of decaying every second, add up to a smooth exponential. A fair coin tossed enough times settles on one half. Both are the law of large numbers, and both show how fast the noise shrinks.
          
        
          
        
          decayP(atom survives t) = e^−λt   ⇒   E[N(t)] = N₀e^−λt
coinp̂ₙ = heads / n,   sd(p̂ₙ) = √(p(1−p)/n)
          
      
          
      
          
        
          
        
          
          
          
          
          
            
            
          
          
          
          
            
        
            
      
            
    
            

    
    
            
      
            
        
            
          
            Stochastic processes · random walks
            
          
            Where a random walker ends up
            
          
            Each step goes right with probability p and left otherwise. The average position drifts by 2p − 1 per step, while the spread grows only like √n. In two dimensions the same √n law sets how far a walker strays from home.
            
        
            
        
            1-DE[Xₙ] = n(2p − 1)      Var(Xₙ) = 4np(1 − p)
2-D, 8 directionsE[Rₙ²] = 1.5n           E[Rₙ] ≈ √(3πn/8)
            
      
            
      
            
        
            
        
            
          
            
          
            
            
            
          
            
          
          
              
        
              
      
              
    
              
  
              

  
              
    
              How it's built
              
    
              Solver
              A fixed-step fourth-order Runge–Kutta integrator written from scratch drives every ODE model, with dose events and lockdown switches handled between steps.
              
    
              Verified
              The solver is checked live against the Bass closed form and the rumour model's conserved quantity. The errors are printed in the readouts.
              
    
              Stochastic engines
              Seeded chain-binomial epidemic, exact geometric decay times, and Monte Carlo walks, each compared with its analytic mean and variance.
              
    
              Data
              Influenza in an English boarding school, January 1978, reported in the British Medical Journal. β and α are fitted by least squares in the browser.
