Epidemics, 3D shapes and stock markets, built on one numerical toolkit

A handful of methods do most of the work in scientific computing: ODE solvers, Monte Carlo, eigen-decompositions and least squares. This project puts three of my builds on that shared core. Then it turns the epidemic and geometry tools on 33 years of real market data, to find out what they see that a forecasting model misses.

Markets through the other two lenses

The Markets pillar asks whether a model can forecast tomorrow. This study asks three different questions, each with a tool built for another field. Data: daily SPY, VIX and five large stocks (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, JPM, XOM), , adjusted prices from Yahoo Finance.

Variance ratio

A random walk's k-day variance is k times its 1-day variance, a ratio of 1. SPY's ratio falls below 1, which means small reversals. The grey band is where 95% of shuffled histories land.

Memory: autocorrelation of returns and of their size

A random walk has no memory in either. Returns have almost none. Their size has memory that lasts months: calm follows calm and storms follow storms.

C · ShapeLab

Do market crashes have a shape?

ShapeLab builds a statistical model of jawlines: align the outlines, then find the few directions they vary in. The same recipe works on crashes. Each completed drawdown runs from its peak back to that peak. Stretch time to 0–1, scale the depth to 1, and sample 48 landmarks. Rotation is left out, because time and price can't be swapped.

Limits of this study

The variance-ratio test is significant on 1993–2026 as a whole, but weakest in the most recent decade (see the note under the chart). Treat it as a description of the past, not a trading signal.

The dose model is first-order clearance toward a fixed median. Mathematically it is a thresholded AR(1) process, and the pharmacology is an analogy that happens to fit, not a mechanism.

Crash shapes include only drawdowns that recovered, which leaves out stocks that never came back. All six stocks are large companies that exist today.
